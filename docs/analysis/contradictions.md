## 1. IPDA: "algorithm" vs "explanatory model"

**Transcripts (video "Trading institucional II" and "Cómo hacen trading los market makers"):**
> "No existe tal cosa como IPDA. IPDA es un modelo explicativo que se utiliza para
> saber cómo funciona el resto de participantes del mercado con respecto a un modelo
> específico de negocio. No existe un algoritmo que conecte todos los bancos del
> mundo [...] son muchísimos algoritmos trabajando al mismo tiempo."

**Current `technical.yaml`:**
Uses `IPDA` as a synonym for the four-phase market framework
(consolidation / expansion / retracement / regression) and as the "price delivery
algorithm".

**Resolution in Phase 1:**
- Keep the **four-phase framework** under its current name.
- Add a new section (`interbank_reality`) clarifying that IPDA/ebitda is an
  *explanatory model* not a literal unified algorithm. Each bank runs its own
  algorithms; they react to each other through aggregators and liquidity
  providers; correlated behavior emerges from shared incentives, not central
  coordination.
- Flag that any future code that references "the IPDA algorithm" should be
  treated as a heuristic abstraction, never as an assumption that institutional
  players share state.

> son los que siguen la tendencia."

> dinero institucional se sale (vía retrocesos) esperamos un pullback del 75-86%

**Current `technical.yaml`:**
- `order_blocks: Points where there was sponsorship of an institutional movement.`
- `mitigation_blocks: candles that go against the current move — unfilled
  institutional orders.`

**Resolution:**

  footprints* of institutional flow and still valid as zones.
- Add a note that "institutional sponsorship" is not a one-shot event: the 86%
  impulse is generated by institutional money that then *withdraws* at the
  confluence, leaving the 14% retail flow — which is exactly why the retracement
  target is 75–86%, not 50%.
- Clarify that commercials on COT are *defensive/hedging*, not trend drivers.

  venue spread.
- The new field `retracement_entry_zones` replaces the single 75% number without
  deleting it.

## 5. "Price vs market" semantic contradiction

**Transcripts (multiple):**
> "El mercado no sube ni baja. El precio sube o baja. El mercado es un lugar
> donde se compra y se vende algo a un bid y a un ask."
>
> "El precio se mueve verticalmente, no diagonalmente. La diagonalidad la da el
> tiempo, no el precio. Por eso las trendlines no funcionan."

**Current `technical.yaml`:**
Uses "market" and "price" loosely ("market moves via orders", "price movement",
etc.).

**Resolution:**
- This is primarily a **semantic** distinction, not a computational one. For
  the purposes of the code, "price going up" and "market going up" are treated
  as the same thing at the timeframe granularity we operate on.
- Added a short `semantics` section in `technical.yaml` acknowledging the
  distinction so that future documentation doesn't drift.
- **AMBIGUITY PRESERVED:** Does "price moves vertically" mean trendlines should
  be *disallowed* in the backtest engine, or merely *deprioritized*? The
  transcripts say both. For Phase 1, we mark trendlines as **not a standalone
  signal** but still useful as visual context.
